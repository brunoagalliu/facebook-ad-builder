# Angle Selection and Identification

Drawn from Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising* (channeling desire, identification) and Halbert's "starving crowd" principle. This governs *which* angle to commit to, before any hook or framework is chosen.

## Copy channels desire, it doesn't create it

The reader already wants something — relief from a pain, a specific outcome, a version of themselves they're trying to become. This engine's job is never to manufacture a new desire; it's to find the strongest desire that already exists in the given CustomerProfile and point the whole ad at it. An ad built on a desire the reader doesn't already have will underperform one built on a weaker offer aimed at a desire they already feel intensely.

## The starving crowd principle (Halbert)

Halbert's famous claim: given a choice between a great sales letter to a lukewarm audience and a weak letter to a starving one, take the starving crowd every time — market fit beats copy quality. This engine doesn't choose the audience (the CustomerProfile is a fixed input), but the principle still applies to *how* that profile is used: treat the given pain_points and goals as if this is the most acute version of that need the reader has ever felt, and commit fully to it. Softening or genericizing the angle to appeal more broadly always weakens response — it trades a strong reaction from the real audience for a weak reaction from everyone.

## Identification

The reader acts when they see themselves as the protagonist of the copy, not when they're told about a generic customer. Use the specific situation implied by the profile's demographics/pain_points/goals rather than a generalized "you" — the closer the copy's imagined scene matches the reader's actual moment (what they're doing, what they're frustrated by, right now), the more the copy reads as written for them specifically rather than at an audience segment.

## Practical rule for this engine

Before generating, pick the single strongest pain point or goal from the profile and build the variation around that one thing — not a blended, hedge-every-angle summary of all of them. Different variations should commit to different single angles (a different pain point, or the pain vs. the aspiration), not the same angle restated in different words.
