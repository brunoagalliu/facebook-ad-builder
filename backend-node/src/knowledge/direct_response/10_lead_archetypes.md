# Lead Archetypes

Drawn from Michael Masterson and John Forde's *Great Leads*. Where `01_hooks_and_headlines.md` covers the four psychological levers that earn a stop, this covers distinct *shapes* an opening can take — a vocabulary of lead types to rotate through so variations differ in structure, not just wording.

- **Offer Lead** — opens with the deal itself ("50% off ends tonight"). Works when the offer alone is strong enough to be the hook — a weak offer exposed this early has nowhere to hide.
- **Promise Lead** — opens by stating the single biggest benefit as a direct promise ("Walk pain-free in 2 weeks"). The workhorse lead for a clear, believable, singular outcome.
- **Problem-Solution Lead** — opens by naming the problem vividly, with the solution implied but not yet stated. Different from PAS as a body structure (`02_body_copy_frameworks.md`) — this is specifically about what the *first line* does, independent of how the rest of the copy is built.
- **Secret/Curiosity Lead** — opens by implying there's a hidden mechanism or fact the reader doesn't know yet. Only works when the payoff is real and delivered soon after (see the curiosity-lever caveat in `01_hooks_and_headlines.md`).
- **Proclamation Lead** — a bold, news-style declarative statement ("Announcing...", "Finally..."). Best for genuine novelty; reads as hype if the news isn't actually new.
- **Story Lead** — opens mid-scene, in the middle of a moment, rather than with a claim at all. Needs room to land — pairs with Star-Story-Solution or Before-After-Bridge (`02`), not a compressed bullet format.
- **Editorial Lead** — opens like a news or feature-story headline rather than an ad ("Local Trainer Discovers Why Most Joint Supplements Don't Work"). This is the lead type most native/advertorial-style affiliate ads use — see `12_advertorial_and_native_craft.md`.

## Practical rule for this engine

Rotate through different lead types across variations rather than defaulting to the same shape every time (Promise Lead is the easy default — resist always reaching for it). Match the lead type to the market-sophistication guidance already in `01_hooks_and_headlines.md`: skeptical/sophisticated audiences respond better to Secret or Editorial leads that break pattern, while unaware audiences respond better to a direct Problem-Solution or Promise lead that doesn't assume any prior context.
