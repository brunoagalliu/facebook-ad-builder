# Primal Desires (Life-Force 8)

Drawn from Drew Eric Whitman's *Ca$hvertising*. This is a different psychological layer than `03_psychological_triggers.md` — Cialdini's triggers describe *why people say yes* in a persuasion interaction (social proof, authority, reciprocity); the Life-Force 8 describes wants that exist in the reader *before* any ad is ever seen. The ad doesn't create these wants, it connects the product to one that's already there — the same principle as "channeling, not inventing" desire in `06_angle_and_identification.md`, at a more primal, pre-rational level.

## The 8 primal wants

1. Survival, enjoyment of life, life extension
2. Enjoyment of food and beverages
3. Freedom from fear, pain, and danger
4. Sexual companionship
5. Comfortable living conditions
6. To be superior, winning, keeping up with others
7. Care and protection of loved ones
8. Social approval

## 9 secondary (learned) wants

Weaker than the Life-Force 8 but still real: to be informed, curiosity, cleanliness, efficiency, convenience, dependability/quality, expression of beauty and style, economy/profit, and finding bargains. These work well as supporting bullets once a Life-Force 8 want anchors the main hook — they rarely carry an ad on their own.

## Practical rule for this engine

Identify which Life-Force 8 want the product most directly serves — often indirectly. A joint supplement isn't really selling "comfort," it's selling freedom from pain (#3) and staying competitive/active enough to keep up with peers or grandkids (#6/#7). Use that primal want as the emotional core of the hook and angle (see `06`), then layer 1-2 secondary wants (convenience, economy) into supporting bullets or the offer framing — never lead with a secondary want when a primal one is available and true for the offer.
