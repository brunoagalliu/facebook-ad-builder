# Advertorial and Native Craft

Not drawn from a single book — this is affiliate marketing's own accumulated practice for Meta/TikTok feed placements specifically, and it's the one thing the classic mail-order/print DR canon in this knowledge base has nothing to say about, since none of it was written for a native social feed.

## Disguise as content, not announcement

The best-performing affiliate ad creative reads like something the reader would see in their feed anyway — an editorial headline, a personal story, a friend's recommendation — not a brand talking at them. This is the Editorial Lead from `10_lead_archetypes.md` taken as a default posture rather than one option among several: "Why This Nurse Stopped Recommending Ibuprofen" reads as content; "Introducing JointFlex Pro" reads as an ad and gets scrolled past by a more skeptical, ad-saturated audience.

## First-person and testimonial voice

Writing as a specific person who tried the product ("I was skeptical until...") rather than the brand itself borrows credibility the brand can't claim for itself — a stranger's discovery reads as more trustworthy than a company's claim about its own product. This voice pairs directly with `19_pastor_framework.md` (story-driven structure) and `20_attractive_character.md` (which persona the "I" is).

## Credibility framing native to the format

"As seen on," "doctors are saying," "the secret [group] doesn't want you to know" — these borrow authority (see `03_psychological_triggers.md`) but in a format-specific way: they work because they mimic how real news and social content signals credibility, not how ads do. Use these only when defensible for the actual product/brand — a false "as seen on" claim is a legal and trust risk, not just a style choice.

## Pattern interrupts built for scroll, not for a page or inbox

Direct mail and print DR assume a reader already holding the piece; a feed ad has to first win the scroll itself. That means the hook's job starts one level earlier than headline theory alone covers — an unexpected visual, a jarring first line, or a claim that doesn't sound like every other ad in the category needs to happen before the reader has committed to reading at all.

## Practical rule for this engine

When the campaign format and offer support a longer or story-driven variation, default toward first-person testimonial or editorial framing rather than brand-voice announcement — this is what performs on native/social placements specifically. Reserve a direct brand-voice announcement style for short, high-intent formats (retargeting, a well-known brand's existing audience) where the reader already has context and doesn't need to be convinced this isn't an ad.
