# Headline Audit and Believability

Drawn from Victor Schwab's *How to Write a Good Advertisement*. A practical audit layer for headlines already drafted using `01_hooks_and_headlines.md` and `10_lead_archetypes.md`, plus a rule that qualifies every claim this engine makes.

## The audit questions

A finished headline should be checked against: does it appeal to a real self-interest, does it offer genuine news, does it arouse curiosity that the body copy actually satisfies, and does it promise something quick or easy where that's true. A headline that fails all four is decoration, not a headline — it needs a different angle, not better wording (see the big-idea test in `09_big_idea_and_craft.md`).

## Believability beats cleverness

Schwab's central rule: an impressive claim that isn't believed gets zero credit for its size — the reader simply discounts it as advertising exaggeration and moves on, the same reaction as if the claim were smaller but false. This means a big, true claim often needs to be *scaled down in presentation* to be believed at all: qualifying language ("up to," "in as little as," "most users") keeps a strong claim credible, where a flat, unqualified version of the same claim reads as hype and gets the same discount a lying ad would get.

## How this differs from reason-why (08)

`08_reason_why_and_proof.md` covers *why* a claim is true (the mechanism). This file covers how the claim is *phrased* so it's believed on first read, before the reader even gets to the reason-why. Both matter — a well-qualified claim with no mechanism behind it is honest phrasing wrapped around a hollow promise, and a strong mechanism dressed in an unqualified, too-good claim never gets far enough to have the mechanism read.

## Practical rule for this engine

Before finalizing a headline, check whether the claim as phrased would make a skeptical reader roll their eyes. If the honest, specific version of the claim sounds unbelievable stated flatly, qualify it (a range, a "most," a specific and modest number) rather than either inflating it further or abandoning the claim — a credible, slightly smaller claim will outconvert an impressive one that reads as fake.
