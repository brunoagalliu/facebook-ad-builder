# The Feature-to-Benefit Ladder

Drawn from Bob Bly's *The Copywriter's Handbook*. A concrete extraction technique for turning a raw product description into the emotional benefit that should actually lead the ad — useful because brand/product inputs given to this engine are often plain feature or spec language, not pre-written benefits.

## The "so what?" chain

Take a stated feature and ask "so what does that mean for the reader?" — repeatedly, until the answer stops being a fact about the product and becomes something the reader feels or gets in their actual life. Example: "contains glucosamine and chondroitin" → so what → "supports cartilage" → so what → "less joint pain" → so what → "can get down on the floor and play with grandkids again without wincing." The bottom rung of that ladder is the real ad copy; the top rung is usually just a supporting detail.

## Where each rung belongs

The bottom-rung emotional payoff belongs in the headline or hook — it's what the reader actually wants. The original feature belongs in the body, functioning as the reason-why (`08_reason_why_and_proof.md`) that makes the payoff believable, not as the opening claim itself. Leading with the feature ("glucosamine and chondroitin formula") instead of the payoff is the most common way flat, unconvincing copy gets written directly from a spec sheet.

## Practical rule for this engine

When the product description supplied is mostly feature/spec language, don't copy it into the headline or hook directly. Run it through the ladder at least twice — further if the first "so what" is still abstract — before writing the opening line, then use the original feature language later in the body as support for the claim, not as the claim itself.
