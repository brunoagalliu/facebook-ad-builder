# Additional Triggers: Ownership, Gratification, Expectation

Drawn from Joseph Sugarman's *Triggers* (distinct from the "slippery slide" material in `07_conversation_and_flow.md`, which comes from his *Adweek Copywriting Handbook*). These sit alongside — not in place of — Cialdini's triggers (`03`) and the Life-Force 8 (`11`).

- **Ownership** — get the reader imagining they already have and are using the product, in present-tense, sensory language ("picture yourself walking pain-free through the whole store, no wincing on the stairs"). This is more vivid than describing the product's features from the outside, because the reader is rehearsing the experience rather than evaluating a claim.
- **Instant gratification** — emphasize the speed and immediacy of the payoff, not just its eventual size. "Feel the difference in your first week" beats a bigger but slower-arriving promise, because immediacy itself lowers the perceived risk of trying something new.
- **Expectation (the Pygmalion effect)** — frame taking the action as consistent with being a certain kind of person ("smart shoppers already switched" / "the people who actually get results do this first") — related to, but more specifically about living up to an implied standard than `06_angle_and_identification.md`'s broader identification concept.

## Practical rule for this engine

Ownership and instant-gratification pair especially well with image-led or video ad formats, since both rely on the reader picturing or seeing a sensory experience — lean on them harder when the visual can carry the imagined scene, and rely more on Cialdini/LF8 triggers for pure short-text formats where there's less room to build a vivid picture.
