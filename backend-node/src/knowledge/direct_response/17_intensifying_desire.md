# Intensifying Desire

A deeper cut from Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising*, beyond the market-sophistication material already in `01_hooks_and_headlines.md` and the channeling/identification material in `06_angle_and_identification.md`.

## The full anatomy: attention → identification → intensification → close

Schwartz's sequence: the headline earns attention, identification (`06`) connects the reader to the desire as their own, but the copy still has to *intensify* that desire — build it to its peak — before the close (`04_offer_and_cta.md`) can channel it into action. Skipping straight from identification to the offer is the most common reason a technically correct ad still reads as flat or salesy: the reader hasn't been given room to want the outcome more before being asked to act.

## How intensification actually works

Not by repeating the claim louder, but by dramatizing the payoff in specific, sensory terms — what changes in the reader's actual day, what they'll notice, what someone else will say or see. Contrast is the main tool: hold the current frustration and the vivid future side by side for a beat before moving to the offer, so the gap between them is what's driving the reader forward, not just the promise in isolation.

## Practical rule for this engine

For longer-form variations (Before-After-Bridge or Star-Story-Solution from `02_body_copy_frameworks.md`), include one explicit intensification beat — a specific, sensory visualization of the payoff — between the hook/identification and the offer, rather than moving straight from problem to deal. Short bullet-format copy can't fit a full beat, but should still compress it into one vivid, specific modifier per bullet rather than a flat feature statement.
