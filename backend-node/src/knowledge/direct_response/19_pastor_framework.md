# The PASTOR Framework

Drawn from Ray Edwards' *How to Write Copy That Sells*. An alternative full body-copy structure to the ones in `02_body_copy_frameworks.md`, purpose-built for first-person testimonial and story-driven copy rather than compressed product-benefit logic.

## The structure

**P**roblem — name it precisely, same as PAS. **A**mplify — agitate the cost of the problem, same intent as PAS's agitate step and reinforced by the intensification technique in `17_intensifying_desire.md`. **S**tory — a specific person's account of finding the solution, told as a real narrative with a beginning and a turning point, not a summary. **T**ransformation/testimony — the concrete outcome that resulted, stated as evidence, not just claimed. **O**ffer — the deal. **R**esponse — a specific, unambiguous instruction for what to do next (see `04_offer_and_cta.md`'s CTA construction).

## How this differs from PAS

PAS compresses a problem-to-solution logic into 2-3 lines and works for short, punchy formats. PASTOR needs enough room for an actual story with a specific character and a turning point — it doesn't compress into a bullet-driven or feed-caption-length format. Use it when the format and voice call for a genuine narrative, not as a longer version of PAS for its own sake.

## Practical rule for this engine

Reserve PASTOR for variations written in first-person testimonial or advertorial voice (see `12_advertorial_and_native_craft.md` and `20_attractive_character.md`) where there's room for a real story — a specific person, a specific moment they tried the product, a specific result. Don't force PASTOR's story/testimony steps into a short bullet-format variation; use PAS or bullet-driven stacking (`02`) there instead.
