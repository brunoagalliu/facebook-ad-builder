# Hooks and Headlines

The headline's only job is to get the very next line read. It does not need to summarize the offer — it needs to make stopping mandatory. Drawn from Caples, Sugarman, Schwartz, and Ogilvy's headline work.

## The four levers that reliably stop a scroll

1. **Self-interest** — a direct, specific benefit stated plainly. "Lose the back pain that's kept you off the golf course." Specific beats generic: name the exact pain, the exact outcome, the exact timeframe.
2. **Curiosity** — an open loop the reader needs closed. Works only when it's honest and the payoff exists in the body copy — a curiosity hook with no payoff reads as clickbait and kills trust for the rest of the ad.
3. **News** — genuine novelty ("new," "just released," "finally") signals the reader hasn't seen this solution before, which matters most for markets that are skeptical or over-pitched.
4. **Fear/urgency of loss** — what happens if they don't act. Loss aversion is consistently a stronger driver than equivalent gain-framing; use it when the cost of inaction is real and specific, not manufactured.

## Practical formulas (Schwartz's "4 U's" plus classic direct-response patterns)

- **Useful** — a clear, tangible benefit.
- **Urgent** — a reason it matters today, not eventually.
- **Unique** — something the reader hasn't heard framed this way before.
- **Ultra-specific** — a number, a name, a timeframe. "Cut 3 inches" beats "cut inches."

Common headline shapes: direct benefit ("Get X without Y"), how-to ("How to X in [timeframe]"), question that names the exact pain, "the [surprising fact] that [authority figure] doesn't want you to know," before/after framing, and the callout ("If you [specific trait/situation], read this").

## Market awareness changes the right hook (Schwartz)

Match the hook to how much the audience already knows:
- **Unaware** they have the problem → lead with the problem itself, made vivid.
- **Problem-aware, solution-unaware** → lead with "there's a way to fix this" and the mechanism.
- **Solution-aware, not product-aware** → lead with why this specific approach/mechanism is different or better.
- **Product-aware** → lead with the offer, the proof, or the deal — they already believe, they just need a reason to buy from you, today.
- **Most sophisticated/skeptical** → lead with something that breaks pattern from every other ad they've seen in this space (a new mechanism claim, an against-the-grain angle), because generic claims have already been burned into worthlessness for this reader.

Getting this stage wrong is the most common reason a strong-sounding headline underperforms — it's answering a question the reader isn't asking yet.
