# Objection Handling

The hesitation that kills a click almost never gets voiced — it just shows up as a scroll past. Good direct-response copy answers the objection before the reader fully forms it. Drawn from Schwartz's market-sophistication framework and Halbert's objection-preemption technique.

## The three objections present in nearly every ad
1. **"Will this actually work for me?"** — answered by proof (specifics, numbers, social proof, before/after) placed near the claim it supports, not isolated in a separate section.
2. **"Is this worth the money/effort/time?"** — answered by value framing: compare to a familiar cost the reader already accepts ("less than a coffee a day"), or stack the offer (see `04_offer_and_cta.md`) so the value clearly exceeds the ask.
3. **"What if I'm wrong to trust this?"** — answered by risk reversal (guarantee, trial, easy exit) placed at the point of decision, not earlier where it can be forgotten.

## Matching objection-handling to sophistication (Schwartz)
- Early-stage/unaware markets have fewer built-in objections — the main risk is disbelief that a solution exists at all. Lead with proof that the problem is solvable.
- Sophisticated markets have specific, accumulated objections from every competitor's ad they've already seen ("I've tried things like this before and they didn't work"). For these, generic proof is not enough — the copy needs to name the specific reason past solutions failed and explain what's mechanically different this time.

## Practical rule for this engine
When generating copy, identify the single most likely objection for this specific offer/audience pairing and make sure at least one variation answers it directly and explicitly — not just implicitly through tone. An ad that only ever states benefits, never addressing why a skeptical reader might not believe them, will underperform against one that spends even one line pre-empting the doubt.
